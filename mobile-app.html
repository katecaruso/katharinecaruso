Mobile App Development

For this project, I revisited the Friends trivia quiz I created for an earlier web design assignment and transformed it into a mobile application using MIT App Inventor. With AI guidance, I explored visual programming, mobile interface design, and the process of developing and testing an interactive application.

The One with the Friends Trivia Quiz

How well do you know the gang?

Test your knowledge of the iconic television series with ten multiple-choice questions, playful feedback, live score tracking, and a final score. Think you know everything about Friends?

Download Android App (.apk)

The APK file is intended for Android devices. I tested the application on my iPhone using the MIT App Inventor Companion.

View the Original Web-Based Trivia Quiz

Development Process

This was my first experience using MIT App Inventor. I began in the Designer interface, organizing labels, buttons, and a vertical arrangement. I used a lavender-and-purple color palette, colorful dots inspired by the show's logo, and familiar references from the series to give the app a playful personality.

I used AI to guide me through the Blocks editor, where I worked with variables, lists, procedures, conditional statements, and event handlers. Organizing and nesting the blocks so that the questions, answers, and scoring worked together correctly was one of the more challenging aspects of the project.

The completed application includes:

  • Ten multiple-choice trivia questions.
  • Immediate feedback for correct and incorrect answers.
  • Live score tracking and question progress.
  • Logic that prevents duplicate scoring.
  • A final results screen.
  • The option to restart the quiz.

Testing and Usability

I tested the application on my iPhone using the MIT App Inventor Companion. I checked correct and incorrect responses, score tracking, question progression, final results, and the restart feature.

One thing that surprised me was how different the app looked on my iPhone compared to what I was seeing in the Designer. I went back and forth between the two several times to adjust font size, spacing, contrast, and button placement. Testing it on my phone let me fix appearance problems that a green checkmark from a working flow wouldn't have revealed.

Once I was satisfied with the results, I used App Inventor to build the Android APK file and hosted it on GitHub Pages.

AI-Assisted Development and Reflection

My experience with PowerApps made some aspects of MIT App Inventor feel familiar, particularly the relationship between the visual interface and the logic behind it. However, I relied heavily on AI guidance when learning how to organize the programming blocks.

This project gave me an opportunity to explore how AI can support the development process while still requiring me to make design decisions, test functionality, and evaluate the user experience. Understanding how an application works remains important when troubleshooting and refining what AI helps us create.

Moving forward, I would like to explore more advanced mobile app development concepts and how AI can support the creation of more complex interactive learning experiences for both Android and iOS devices.

Overall, it was fun to take something I had previously designed for the web and turn it into a mobile application.
